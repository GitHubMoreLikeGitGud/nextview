      What it does

      NextView calibrates a camera by deciding where the calibration board should go next. Each step it looks at the new frame with OpenCV 5 tools, rejects bad frames with a measured reason (blur measured on the board's own edges, glare, a board too small or not found), refits the camera model, maps the per-pixel uncertainty of the calibration over the whole image, and asks for the board pose that is predicted to shrink that uncertainty the most. It stops when the predicted full-image error is below target, and it asks a person before it changes the lens model or exports a calibration.

      How a step works

      1. Perceive: ChArUco detection (CharucoDetector), slanted-edge blur and glare checks, planar pose with IPPE (solvePnPGeneric).
      2. Decide: a priority-ordered rule table (R0–R13) over the measurements; refit with calibrateCameraExtended (rational model or calibrateCameraROExtended only after approval); full intrinsic covariance from projectPoints Jacobians; a stop probability P(true field p95 > τ).
      3. Act: the next target as a ghost outline plus a numeric instruction (or a robot pose), chosen from ranked candidates by predicted reduction of the 95th-percentile uncertainty.

      Every rule writes one trace line, so the trace is the audit record of the session.

      Privacy and limits

      • Webcam frames are decoded in memory for one step and never stored or logged.
      • The session lives in a signed token held by your browser; it expires after 6 hours. Approved exports are written to a private bucket; download links expire after an hour and the files are deleted after 30 days.
      • Pinhole and rational lens models only (fisheye lenses are detected and declined). Rolling shutter is not modelled. The uncertainty is first order; the stop probability is fitted in simulation and transferred to real cameras.
      • The service is rate limited; if it is busy the page retries automatically.
